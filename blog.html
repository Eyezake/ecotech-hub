How to recycle lithium-ion batteries, phone accessories, and small gadgets

Small tech is easy to collect and surprisingly easy to mishandle. A phone, power bank, wireless earbud case, smartwatch, camera, controller, or laptop may contain a rechargeable lithium-ion battery. Chargers, cables, keyboards, and other accessories may not contain batteries, but they are still electrical products that should go through an electronics take-back or recycling route rather than being abandoned in a drawer or placed in the wrong bin.

Start by separating battery-powered items

Keep devices with built-in batteries separate from loose cables and accessories. Do not put lithium-ion batteries or devices containing them in household garbage or a municipal recycling bin. Batteries can be crushed or damaged during collection and sorting, creating a fire risk and putting workers and facilities in danger. They also contain materials that can be recovered instead of lost.

Prepare a phone or gadget before handover

Back up the information you want to keep, sign out of accounts, remove SIM and memory cards, and use the device’s factory-reset process. If the battery is removable, follow the product and collection program instructions. EPA guidance recommends placing each battery or battery-containing device in a separate plastic bag and covering exposed terminals with non-conductive tape, such as electrical tape, to help prevent contact and sparks. Always check the receiving program’s instructions before transporting a battery.

Choose the right destination

Use a manufacturer take-back program, a participating retailer, a certified electronics recycler, or a local household hazardous-waste or battery collection program. Ask whether the location accepts the specific item: loose rechargeable batteries, power banks, phones, earbuds, chargers, and cables may be collected through different streams. Keep accessories together only when the program says it is helpful; otherwise, separate them so each material can be processed correctly.

Stop if a battery is damaged

A swollen, punctured, leaking, hot, smoking, or recalled battery needs special handling. Do not charge it, use it, puncture it, place it in a normal collection box, or mail it without specific instructions. Keep people and flammable materials away if you can do so safely, then contact the manufacturer, local hazardous-waste program, or emergency services according to the situation. Damaged batteries should never be treated like ordinary recycling.

A simple rule: keep battery-powered gadgets out of both household bins, protect the battery terminals when the collection program instructs you to, and confirm the destination before you travel. See the U.S. EPA consumer guidance and Call2Recycle safety resources for current handling details. Local rules vary, so follow the instructions of the program accepting your device.
